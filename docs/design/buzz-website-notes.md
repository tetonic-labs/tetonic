# Buzz website: design reference for Tetonic

Reviewed September 24, 2026. Source: [buzz.xyz](https://buzz.xyz/).

Scope: desktop homepage, scrolling through the product presentation, feature cards, signup section, closing call to action, and opening the download menu. These are visual observations and design judgments, not a performance or accessibility audit. Mobile behavior, reduced-motion behavior, and form submission were not tested.

## What makes the design work

### 1. A recognizable visual language

The bee symbol appears in the corner branding, floating decorative elements, and character illustrations. A fine background pattern, soft gradients, rounded surfaces, and oversized lettering repeat throughout the page.

**Why it works:** the details feel related. The identity survives even when the wordmark is off-screen. There is personality at both the page scale and the small-detail scale.

**For Tetonic:** give our symbol a small, consistent supporting role beyond the header and footer. It could inform diagram markers or section accents. Keep the warm paper, ink, and copper palette. Avoid scattering the logo across every available surface; repetition needs restraint.

### 2. Large changes in scale create hierarchy

The opening brand treatment is enormous, while supporting labels and controls stay compact. Later headings are large without making all the body copy heavy. Generous open space separates the main ideas.

**Why it works:** a visitor can identify the focal point before reading. The page gives important statements room instead of making every element compete.

**For Tetonic:** preserve a strong display headline, but pair it with a short, concrete description of the product. Use fewer competing eyebrow labels and secondary statements near the hero. Let the product preview become the next major focal point.

### 3. The page has a deliberate sequence

The homepage moves from brand and proposition to product explanation and an interface presentation, then grouped benefits, signup, and a closing action area.

**Why it works:** the visitor receives progressively more useful context. The closing action arrives after the page has explained what the product is for.

**For Tetonic:** use this sequence on individual product pages: purpose → real product view → useful workflows → control and ownership → next action. On the company homepage, introduce the product choice earlier because we have more than one product.

### 4. The product gets a substantial visual stage

The interface is shown at a large scale inside a rounded, contrasting frame. It is given its own section rather than reduced to a decorative thumbnail.

**Why it works:** an interface makes an abstract product claim tangible. A visitor can start to imagine using the software.

**For Tetonic:** this is the highest-value idea to try. Show an authentic Lokai interaction: a request, relevant work, and a visible result or approval decision. A readable still is enough for the first iteration. Use actual available UI; do not invent a polished future interface or unsupported capability.

### 5. Related benefits are easy to compare

Three feature cards use matching proportions, character illustrations, concise headings, and similarly sized text blocks.

**Why it works:** the repeated structure makes the content easy to scan, while distinct illustrations prevent it from feeling mechanical.

**For Tetonic:** organize a small number of workflows into matching treatments. Give each one a specific example or product crop. Avoid a large grid of generic icons and capability labels. Product cards should also make availability differences obvious.

### 6. Navigation leaves room for the story

Compact controls remain available while scrolling. The download control opens a platform menu instead of displaying every platform as a separate top-level button. Its expanded state visibly changes the button treatment.

**Why it works:** the visitor has a persistent next step without a visually heavy navigation bar. Additional choices appear when needed.

**For Tetonic:** keep Products and Docs easy to reach; a single-product site's sparse navigation cannot be transferred directly to our product family. Group platform choices on the appropriate product page, and retain clear unpublished-build messaging until downloads exist.

### 7. Backgrounds connect the sections

The page moves through yellow-green, pale green, and blue backgrounds while retaining its fine pattern. The closing area returns to the strong opening color and oversized brand treatment.

**Why it works:** sections feel distinct but connected. The ending visually recalls the beginning.

**For Tetonic:** test quieter changes in surface color, with a stronger ink section for a major transition. Keep texture subtle enough that body copy and screenshots remain clear. Our existing paper tone can carry this structure without adopting Buzz's palette.

### 8. Motion reinforces the personality

Floating symbols and changing closing copy make the page feel active. The blurred, grainy brand treatment contributes to the same playful character.

**Why it works:** motion is connected to the site's identity rather than feeling like a separate effects layer.

**For Tetonic:** prioritize the small coordinated brand hover we already developed and restrained section transitions. Keep explanatory text stable. Any larger motion experiment should preserve immediate readability and offer a reduced-motion alternative.

## What to borrow cautiously

- Large empty areas create drama but can delay useful information. Judge spacing by how quickly a visitor reaches a product explanation.
- Soft or blurred lettering is distinctive for a brand moment; it is unsuitable for documentation and explanatory copy.
- Animated decoration can compete with the product. Test it at real reading speed, not only in a screenshot.
- The signup form looks simple, but visual simplicity alone does not establish usability. Keep persistent labels, clear errors, and an explicit action label in our forms.
- Buzz's playful characters are specific to its identity. Tetonic needs its own visual material and a tone appropriate to ownership, control, and software work.

## Suggested order of experiments

1. **Add a real Lokai product view.** Check whether a new visitor can explain what they would do with it.
2. **Refine the product-page sequence.** Give each section one question to answer and one clear focal point.
3. **Improve the product-family introduction.** Make Lokai and Mantle distinct while showing their shared foundation and honest availability.
4. **Develop a restrained symbol and diagram vocabulary.** Reuse a few shapes, spacing rules, and copper accents consistently.
5. **Experiment with section backgrounds and motion last.** Keep only changes that improve comprehension or recognition.

The useful lesson is consistency: branding, layout, product imagery, and interaction all support the same story. Apply that discipline to Tetonic's own identity.
